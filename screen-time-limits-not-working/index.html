Why iPhone Screen Time limits don't stop you

If you have set App Limits on your iPhone and still end up scrolling, you are not weak — the feature is designed to let you through.

What happens when a limit runs out

When an app hits its daily limit, iOS shows a block screen. By default it offers choices such as Remind me in 15 minutes or Ignore limit for today. One tap, and the app is back. That is the moment most people give in.

The passcode workaround, and its catch

You can add a Screen Time passcode so the “ignore limit” option asks for it. It helps only if you do not know the passcode. If you set it yourself, you can type it whenever you are tempted. People get around this by asking a friend or family member to set it — which works, but is awkward to set up and to change.

Limits measure time, not intent

A daily limit counts minutes spread across the whole day. It does not stop the 7 a.m. check that turns into 40 minutes, or the scroll while you are meant to be studying. Many people need the apps to be unavailable at specific moments rather than rationed all day.

What works better

  • Block for a set period. A Timer Lock makes your chosen apps unavailable for the next 25 minutes or two hours, with no “ignore for today” button.
  • Block on a schedule. A Schedule Lock applies on the days and hours you pick, such as 9–5 on weekdays, and lifts by itself.
  • Give the key to someone else. With Buddy Lock, the code that ends a lock early goes to your buddy, not to you.

An honest note

Without Buddy Lock, you can end a Timer or Schedule lock yourself — the lock is only as strong as your commitment. That is exactly why Buddy Lock exists. AppTimeOut uses Apple's Screen Time system to enforce blocks, so they keep working when the app is closed.
